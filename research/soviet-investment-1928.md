# Soviet investment at the start of the First Five-Year Plan

**Research date:** 2026-09-03  
**Scope:** USSR, 1928/29 starting year; the First Five-Year Plan (FYP) as originally approved; realized outcomes through 1932/33 where the series is available. Agriculture and grain are excluded from the investment discussion. Monetary figures are nominal Soviet rubles unless a source says otherwise.

## Executive answer

There is no defensible single, source-owned 1928/29 “investment budget” that simultaneously covers every requested sector in cash and physical units. Soviet planning documents separate the annual budget, capital-construction plans, and production-control figures; later historians reconstruct investment with different price bases and coverage. The most supportable answer is therefore a reconciliation:

* The original FYP planned roughly **64.6 billion rubles of total capital investment** over 1928/29-1932/33, in the plan's own prices; the major published non-agricultural blocks were approximately **16.4 billion for industry** and **10.8 billion for transport**. These are five-year plan allocations, not the one-year 1928/29 budget and not foreign direct investment. [Nove, *Economic History*, 3rd ed., pp. 169-72; Allen, *Farm to Factory*, pp. 64-69.]
* The 1928/29 starting program was a rapidly rising first-year appropriation, but the surviving summaries do not provide a clean, additive cash table for all the requested subsectors. The report gives the published starting-year aggregates where they can be identified and labels reconstructed balances rather than presenting them as official line items.
* Physical targets are much better documented. The approved program called for, approximately, coal **35 to 75 million tonnes**, crude oil **11.7 to 22 million tonnes**, pig iron **3.3 to 10 million tonnes**, steel **4.3 to 10.4 million tonnes**, rolled products **3.4 to 7.1 million tonnes**, coke **about 7.8 to 19 million tonnes**, and electricity **about 5.1 to 22 billion kWh**. Actual 1932/33 results were generally below the ambitious targets: coal about **64 million tonnes**, oil about **21 million tonnes**, pig iron about **6.2 million tonnes**, steel about **5.9 million tonnes**, and electricity about **13.5 billion kWh**. The exact series and definitions differ by source, especially for oil, rolled products, and electricity.
* A separate external-resource sensitivity is **5% of 1928 GDP**, explicitly an assumption. Using Allen's reconstructed 1928 GDP of approximately **100 billion 1937 rubles**, this is **about 5 billion 1937 rubles**. It is a hypothetical resource envelope, **not FDI**, not an addition to the Soviet investment budget, and is not allocated by sector.

## Methods and comparability rules

1. **Starting allocation** means the first FYP operating year, 1928/29, not the calendar year 1928 and not the later “optimal” or revised plan.
2. **Original plan** means the control figures approved in 1928-29 for the five-year period. **Realized** means output reported for 1932 or 1932/33, depending on the series.
3. Ruble figures are not converted into modern currency. The plan's current prices, later constant-price reconstructions, state-budget appropriations, and gross capital formation are different objects.
4. A dash means that the sources located do not support an additive cash number for that exact row. It is preferable to a false allocation. “Included in parent” prevents double counting.
5. Physical figures are output or capacity targets, not investment quantities. Infrastructure physical indicators are included only when the cited source reports them; no physical model is invented for residual sectors.
6. The source hierarchy is: contemporary Soviet plan/budget tables; contemporary Soviet statistical series; archival/high-trust reconstructions; scholarly syntheses. Official figures are not treated as neutral measurements of realized performance.

## Cash allocation

### Five-year cash envelope, original plan

| Scope | Planned cash | Status and treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| Total capital investment, all sectors | **64.6 bn rubles** | Official-plan figure as reproduced by Nove and Allen; includes agriculture and other sectors, so it is not used as an agriculture-excluded total. |
| Industry, broad | **~16.4 bn rubles** | Published plan aggregate; includes the industrial branches below, but branch cash lines are not additive in the available summary. |
| Transport, broad | **~10.8 bn rubles** | Published plan aggregate; includes rail and other transport. |
| Agriculture and grain | **Excluded** | The user-requested scope excludes this block; no attempt is made to subtract it using an incompatible reconstruction. |
| Residual non-agricultural investment, one estimated slice | **~27 bn rubles over five years** | **Estimate, low confidence:** broad balance after removing the published industry and transport blocks from the non-agricultural total reconstructed by Nove/Allen. It covers construction, housing, municipal services, communications and unreported branches; it is not an official budget line and must not be added to the official total without checking the reconstruction's coverage. |

The residual is deliberately one slice only. There is no cash allocation below it for housing, municipalities, roads, ports, or other residual sectors because doing so would create an invented model. The broad industry and transport figures also should not be added to the residual without first fixing the exact coverage of the underlying reconstruction.

### 1928/29 starting-year cash

| Requested area | 1928/29 cash finding | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| State investment / capital construction, broad | **about 11-12 bn rubles in contemporary summaries** | Starting-year plan magnitude; budget and capital-construction coverage vary. Treat as an order of magnitude, not a directly comparable annual share of 64.6 bn. |
| Industry, broad | **about 5 bn rubles in contemporary plan summaries** | Includes new industrial construction and expansion; not a branch-level ledger. |
| Transport, broad | **about 2 bn rubles in contemporary plan summaries** | Rail and other transport; not a rail-only number. |
| Ferrous, nonferrous, coal, oil, chemicals, machine-building | **Not separately recoverable as additive cash lines from the cited summary** | Physical targets are reported below; cash must remain “included in industry.” |
| Construction materials, electricity, fuel infrastructure, rail infrastructure, rolling stock, waterways, ports, roads, housing, municipal infrastructure | **Not separately recoverable as additive cash lines** | Some appear as project or physical targets, but not as a common cash classification. |

These starting-year figures are best read as a documented range and classification warning. The contemporary budget's “national economy” and the capital-construction plan do not have identical coverage; state enterprises also used retained earnings and credit. A one-year branch table assembled by combining them would be spurious.

### Documented subsectors and project logic

The plan's industrial logic was vertically linked: coal and coke fed iron and steel; iron, steel and nonferrous metals fed machine-building; electricity, chemicals, cement, timber, railways and worker settlements were enabling infrastructure. The Dnieper hydroelectric project, Kuznetsk and Magnitogorsk iron-and-steel complexes, the expansion of Donbass coal, Baku/Grozny oil, and new machine-building plants are documented projects, but their project budgets are not interchangeable with the sector totals above. This is why the report keeps project evidence in the physical/project column rather than converting it into fabricated branch cash shares.

## External trade-resource sensitivity

This is a counterfactual accounting assumption, not a historical claim about FDI:

$$
R_{trade}=0.05\times GDP_{1928}
$$

Allen's reconstructed Soviet GDP for 1928 is approximately **100 billion 1937 rubles** in the relevant national-income series. Therefore:

$$
R_{trade}\approx0.05\times100=5\text{ billion 1937 rubles}
$$

A reasonable rounding band is **4.5-5.5 billion 1937 rubles** if the GDP reconstruction is rounded to roughly 90-110 billion. This resource is external-trade purchasing power under the stated assumption. It is not FDI, not a grant, not a separate Soviet budget appropriation, and not a reason to assign imported machinery to steel, power, rail, or any other sector. No sector allocation is made.

The trade calculation is also not directly comparable with the plan's nominal-ruble investment totals: it uses a reconstructed constant-price GDP series, while the plan figures use contemporary planning prices and institutional coverage.

## What can and cannot be compared

| Comparison | Safe conclusion | Unsafe conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 1928/29 start vs original FYP | The first year launched a multi-year heavy-industry and infrastructure push; targets imply very high growth rates from a low base. | Treating the first-year budget as exactly one-fifth of the five-year plan. |
| Original FYP vs realized output | Most flagship heavy-industry outputs rose sharply; many ambitious targets were missed, especially iron, steel and power. | Calling every shortfall “underinvestment”: shortages, construction delays, labor, coal quality, reclassification and forced mobilization also mattered. |
| Cash vs physical quantities | Cash identifies planned resource claims; physical indicators show intended capacity/output transformation. | Dividing rubles by tonnes to infer a universal sector investment cost. |
| Soviet official vs reconstructed series | Official plan figures are primary evidence of intent; reconstructions help with national totals and prices. | Treating official targets and reconstructed GDP as a common price/accounting system. |

## Gaps and confidence

* **High confidence:** the broad original-plan total, broad industry/transport blocks, and the direction and approximate magnitude of the flagship physical targets, because these recur in contemporary plan material and high-trust historical syntheses.
* **Medium confidence:** the rounded 1932/33 outcomes, because published Soviet series differ in definitions and historians correct or reinterpret them differently.
* **Low confidence:** the 1928/29 cash ranges and the single residual slice. They are retained to answer the cash question but are explicitly not presented as an official branch ledger.
* The requested sectors of electricity transmission, fuel infrastructure, roads, ports, housing and municipal services need project-level archival budgets or a single archival capital-construction classification to be valued without double counting. The sources used here support their existence and physical/project role, not a reliable standalone cash total.

### Reproducibility note

The monetary and physical numbers above are rounded to the precision supported by the source family. Before using them in a quantitative model, obtain one consistent scan/edition of the official plan and one consistent reconstructed statistical series, then reconcile units, prices, territorial coverage, and whether a value is a plan target, appropriation, gross investment, capacity addition, or annual output. This report intentionally does not manufacture that reconciliation from unlike tables.
