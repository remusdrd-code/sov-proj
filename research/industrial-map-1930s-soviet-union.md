# Industrial Map of the Soviet Union in the 1930s: Best Fit for the Project

**Research date:** 2026-09-05  
**Question:** What map of the Soviet Union from the 1930s is the best fit for a historical industrialization game that needs regional industrial sites, resource geography, rail/transport links, and a 1928-to-1930s progression layer?

## Short answer

The strongest single fit is the 1935 LOC map titled "Tazhelaia Industria SSSR Evropeiskaia Chast'. Predpriiat..." ("Heavy Industry of the USSR, European Part. Enterprises ... as of January 1, 1935"). It is the best match for the project because it explicitly shows industrial enterprises and major heavy-industry facilities in the European USSR, with power stations, fuel industries, metallurgical sites, machine-building and metal-working, chemicals, non-metallic minerals, construction materials, railroads, pipelines, water lines, and high-voltage networks in the same map frame. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

The 1933 capital-construction map is a strong second choice for the progression layer, especially if the team wants a map of projects under construction across the whole USSR. The 1930 Asiatic-part map is useful as a regional supplement for Siberia and Kazakhstan. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

## Why this map works for the project

The Library of Congress guide explicitly describes the 1935 European-USSR heavy-industry sheet as a map of the major heavy industries and enterprises operating or under construction in European Russia as of January 1, 1935. It distinguishes large-scale from standard enterprises and identifies electric power stations; fuel industries; ferrous and non-ferrous metallurgical industries; machine construction and metal-working; chemical industries; non-metallic minerals; construction materials; coal industry trusts; petroleum pipelines; water lines; high-voltage networks; iron ore directorates; standard and narrow-gauge railways; and new industrial construction. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

This combination is unusually close to the game's data needs:

- industrial site layer: actual facilities and enterprise locations;
- resource layer: fuel, coal, ore, metals, chemicals, non-metallic minerals;
- power layer: power stations and high-voltage networks;
- transport layer: railways, pipelines, and water lines;
- progression layer: operating and under-construction projects in a 1930s baseline.

The guide says this map is filed under "Russia -- European -- Industry -- Heavy Industry U.S.S.R. -- 1935 -- 1:5,000,000 -- Russia Cartographic Trust" and that it was published by the All-Union Cartographic Trust and the newspaper "Za Industrializatsiiu." [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

## Candidate maps and how they fit

### 1) 1935 heavy-industry map of European USSR — best fit

Title (as described in the LOC guide): "Tazhelaia Industria SSSR Evropeiskaia Chast'. Predpriiat ia N.K.T.P. na 1 Ianvaria 1935 g."  
Date: 1935  
Scale: 1:5,000,000  
Publisher/issuer: All-Union Cartographic Trust and the newspaper "Za Industrializatsiiu"  
Coverage: European USSR  
Primary description: The map depicts the major heavy industries and enterprises operating or under construction in European Russia as of January 1, 1935. It distinguishes large-scale from standard enterprises and marks electric power stations, fuel industries, metallurgical industries (ferrous and non-ferrous), machine construction and metal-working, chemical industries, non-metallic minerals, construction materials, coal industry trusts, petroleum pipelines, water lines, high-voltage networks, iron ore directorates, standard and narrow-gauge railways, and new industrial construction. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

Why it fits the project:
- It is a true 1930s industrial map, not a later Cold War retrospective.
- It includes both operating and under-construction projects, which is useful for the 1930s progression layer.
- It covers the main industrial and network features needed for the game's map model.

Best use in the project:
- 1930s progression baseline and industrial-site layer.
- Add resource, power, and rail nodes by reading the map symbols against the legend.

### 2) 1933 capital-construction map of the USSR — strong project-progression source

Title (as described in the LOC guide): "Karta Kapital'nogo Stroitel'stva po Piatiletnemy Plana, 1928-29 -- 1932-33: novye fabriki, zavody, elektrostantsii, shakhty, sovkhozy, zhel.-dor. linii, sooruzhaemye v techenie piatiletia"  
Date: 1933  
Coverage: entire USSR, with separate European and Asiatic sheets  
Scale: varies by sheet; the LOC guide says one part is 1:3,000,000 (European Russia) and another is 1:10,400,000 (Asiatic Russia)  
Primary description: This map shows capital construction by way of the Five-Year Plan, with the locations of capital-construction projects throughout the Soviet Union for 1928-29 to 1932-33. It identifies 34 industries, including electrification, oil, mining, metallurgy, automobile, railroad, forestry, chemical, and machine works. It also shows railroad lines, oil lines, collective farms, administrative divisions at the oblast level, and place names. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

Why it fits the project:
- It directly captures the early First Five-Year Plan build-out, which is relevant to a 1928 to 1930s progression model.
- It includes project placement, industrial categories, and network corridors.

Best use in the project:
- Project / construction-queue layer.
- Progression timing for industrial buildout and rail/pipeline expansion.

### 3) 1930 Asiatic USSR map — useful for eastern industrialization and Siberia

Title (as described in the LOC guide): "Pi︠a︡tiletniĭ plan novogo stroitel'stva promyshlennosti SSSR : Karta Aziatskoĭ chasti SSSR"  
Date: 1930  
Coverage: Asiatic part of the USSR  
Primary description: The guide identifies it as a 1930 map of the new industrial-construction five-year plan for the Asiatic part of the USSR and notes that it is held by the National Library of Russia and digitized on the Library of Congress website. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

Why it fits the project:
- It is an early 1930s map that covers the highly relevant eastern industrialization frontier.
- It is helpful for Siberia, Kazakhstan, and regional expansion in the game's historical progression.

Best use in the project:
- Eastern-region industrial baselines and resource-access planning.

## Why the later Commons maps are not the primary recommendation

The Wikimedia Commons category for "Economic maps of the Soviet Union" contains many useful visual references, but its members are a mixed set of dates and collection types rather than a single 1930s baseline. The category includes 1951, 1952, 1954, 1955, and later maps such as the 1951 map titled "U.S.S.R. (Including Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, Tannu Tuva, and Island Possessions) - Economic Regions," which the Commons file metadata identifies as a 1951 map. The file is public domain because it is attributed to the Central Intelligence Agency and the National Archives and Records Administration, but it is a later period map, not a 1928 or 1930s baseline. [Commons file: U.S.S.R. (Including Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, Tannu Tuva, and Island Possessions) - Economic Regions](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:U.S.S.R._%28Including_Latvia,_Lithuania,_Estonia,_Tannu_Tuva,_and_Island_Possessions%29_-_Economic_Regions_-_DPLA_-_768bb3b120b65392dfb4ea422e6b2a95.jpg) [Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Economic_maps_of_the_Soviet_Union)

The same applies to the 1954 machine-tool map and the 1949 waterways map: they are important visual references for later Soviet industrial geography, but they are not the primary source for a 1928-to-1935 game map. [Wikimedia Commons: Principal Waterways of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Principal_Waterways_of_the_Union_of_Soviet_Socialist_Republics_(USSR)_-_DPLA_-_167d6a800dd1caf6fd4ddf60b110ced6.jpg) [Wikimedia Commons: U.S.S.R. - Machine-Tool Producing Plants](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:U.S.S.R._-_Machine-Tool_Producing_Plants_-_DPLA_-_7fd40b11ab91eb550a6a1eeb6e6b4c55.jpg)

## Recommendation for this project

If the project needs one map to anchor the 1930s historical industrial layer, choose the 1935 European-USSR heavy-industry map. It is the closest match to the data model because it combines industrial facilities, power, fuel, metals, chemicals, minerals, and transport in one map and because it is explicitly described as covering a point-in-time industrial status on January 1, 1935. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

If the project needs a second map for the construction/progression layer, choose the 1933 capital-construction map because it specifically tracks Five-Year-Plan capital construction and project locations across the USSR. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

If the project needs a third map for eastern resources and Siberian growth, choose the 1930 Asiatic USSR plan. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)

## Limitations

This research was constrained by access conditions from this environment:

- Direct LOC item pages for individual digitized maps (for example, the item page at https://www.loc.gov/item/2018693938/) returned HTTP 403 during this pass, so the map metadata was taken from the first-party LOC guide page rather than the item-detail JSON record. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)
- Direct DPLA/API queries for map records also returned HTTP 403 from this environment, so I did not assume a catalog record beyond the sources that remained accessible. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)
- The Wikimedia Commons files are useful as visual references, but they mostly represent later maps and therefore should not be used as the baseline map for a 1928-to-1935 game unless the date is verified against the specific file page. [Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Economic_maps_of_the_Soviet_Union)

## Bottom line

For a Soviet industrialization project that starts in 1928 and then transitions through the 1930s, the best map is the 1935 European USSR heavy-industry map, with the 1933 capital-construction map as the preferred companion for construction and progression history. Those are the two strongest primary-source candidates from the LOC guide because they are explicitly contemporary to the period, industrial in focus, and rich enough to support site, resource, power, and transport layers. [LOC Industry guide](https://guides.loc.gov/russia-maps/industry)
